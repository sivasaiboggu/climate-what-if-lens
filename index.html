What this is, and what it is not

Read this before you judge anything on screen.

  • Heat is real model data. Open-Meteo Climate API, CMIP6 models CMCC_CM2_VHR4 and EC_Earth3P_HR averaged, ~10 km resolution, daily 1950–2050.
  • Flood is a screening estimate. Bathtub method: land below the scenario water level, from 90 m elevation data. No hydrodynamic modelling.
  • Scenario water levels are planning levels for a severe event (2025: 1 m; 2030: 1 / 2 m; 2040: 2 / 3 m; 2050: 2 / 4 m for SSP2-4.5 / SSP5-8.5): IPCC AR6 global-mean sea-level rise to 2050 (~0.2–0.3 m) plus a local high-tide / storm-surge allowance. Not a mean sea-level projection, and not a local projection. Flooded cells are shaded by depth: pale blue is shallow, dark blue is deep.

Sources

  • Heat: Open-Meteo Climate API (climate-api.open-meteo.com/v1/climate), CMIP6, daily 1950–2050, ~10 km, bias-corrected vs ERA5. Models: CMCC_CM2_VHR4, EC_Earth3P_HR.
  • Elevation: Open-Meteo Elevation API (90 m SRTM-derived), 10 × 10 grid per city.
  • Geocoding: OpenStreetMap Nominatim — max 1 request/second, results cached, no autocomplete (usage policy).
  • Sea level context: IPCC AR6 global-mean rise to 2050, ~0.2–0.3 m across SSPs.
  • Basemap data © OpenStreetMap contributors (schematic rendering; no tiles loaded).
